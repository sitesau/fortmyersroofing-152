Repair vs Replacement

When a repair is still the sensible path A repair is usually the cleaner option when the problem is localised and the rest of the roof still has service life left. The source material specifically lists leaks, missing shingles, cracked tiles and worn flashing as repairable situations, provided the inspection supports that narrower scope. Material type changes what a repair looks like. An asphalt shingle roof can need attention to storm exposure and ventilation. Concrete and clay tile roofs may involve tile repair or re-bedding. Metal jobs can centre on panels and wind performance. Low-slope areas over additions or lanais often need membrane thinking rather than shingle thinking, with TPO, EPDM or modified bitumen named on the source page. For a homeowner comparing quotes, the practical question is whether the contractor has tied the repair to the actual failed component, such as flashing, tile, shingles or a membrane section, instead of defaulting straight to replacement.
